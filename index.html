How an SOS gets out with no network

A browser cannot form a Bluetooth mesh or make calls without a network. So ReliefReady uses the paths that do work on every ordinary phone, and turns aid drivers into couriers for messages from dead zones.

Trapped person

SOS app on any phone

Needs, head-count, infants, injured and GPS (GPS works without data). Saved on the phone first, so nothing is lost. Hindi and English.

Four ways out, tried in order
1InternetSent at once. If there is no data it is queued and sends itself when any signal returns.
2SMS, 1 bar of 2GA 160-character code holds the whole request. The control room reads it automatically.
3Bluetooth / Quick SharePasses the SOS to a nearby phone with no network. Whoever reaches signal first forwards it.
4QR to a rescuerDrivers scan it offline and carry it out (store-and-forward).
+Call 112, light and whistleEmergency calls use any operator's tower in range. The screen flashes SOS for helicopters.

One loop, not five tools

A trapped family's SOS becomes a ranked request, then a driver's stop with quantities, then a delivery, and the family's phone shows “Aid delivered”. The control room never re-types anything between systems.

Offline without special hardware

SMS, 112, Bluetooth hand-off and QR work on any phone people already own. Bridgefy needs the app on every phone in the chain, and Meshtastic needs radio hardware.

Drivers carry messages out

Trucks move between dead zones and coverage every day. The driver app scans SOS codes offline and uploads them on reaching signal, so messages get out on vehicles that are already making the trip.

Decisions a coordinator can defend

Every allocation shows its score breakdown, the quantity short and why (stock, truck time, or no road). A blocked route or a new village reruns the plan and shows who gained and who lost.

Roads judged by the people on them

Real road routes from OpenStreetMap are checked against drivers' live reports of bridges down, flooding and landslides. The safest option is picked first, and unsafe-only routes trigger a boat or airlift request.

A paper trail upward

Requests to the state EOC, NDRF or air support carry a timeline (sent, acknowledged, dispatched, resolved), a “forward higher” step when nobody answers, and a one-click situation report for the official channel.

Limits and next steps

What the prototype does not do yet, and how we would extend it.

Bluetooth mesh and offline calling

Browsers cannot advertise Bluetooth or relay between phones, so a true mesh or walkie-talkie voice needs a small Android companion app (Nearby Connections over Bluetooth and Wi-Fi Direct). It would reuse the same RR1 code.

Zero-network valleys

For places with no tower at all, LoRa mesh radios (Meshtastic) reach several kilometres per hop. A gateway node at the control room would read RR1 codes straight into this inbox.

Real back end

The demo syncs between tabs in one browser, or between viewers of the hosted preview. Production needs a database such as Firebase or Supabase, phone OTP sign-in and an SMS gateway number.
